#### Analog Electronic Computation of Stellar Structure: Polytropic Benchmarks, Circuit Scaling, and Error-Controlled Verification
 - **Authors:** Zuhoor Elahi, Wafa Gull, Christopher Sirola
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30230

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30230

 - **Abstract**
 We present an electronic analog-computing framework for static stellar-structure equations and verify it using polytropic Lane-Emden benchmarks with known or high-accuracy reference solutions. Starting from an exploratory operational-amplifier and multiplier circuit, we identify sign, coefficient, central-regularity, and signal-conditioning problems that complicate direct electronic implementation. We develop dimensionless first-order formulations, derive regular finite-radius initial conditions, and construct voltage-scaled analog architectures with controlled signal headroom. The principal formulation uses a positive derivative state and reproduces the surface locations and dimensionless mass coefficients of the n = 0, 1, and 3 polytropes at the ideal scaled-voltage level. A common circuit architecture accommodates all three benchmarks, while the nonlinear n = 3 case demonstrates how voltage scaling and computation-speed adjustments can preserve the governing equations. Sensitivity analysis and Monte Carlo simulations quantify the propagation of calibration uncertainties into stellar observables. A separate regularized n = 1 circuit, implemented using AD734 and OPAx197 vendor macromodels, passes all 67 deterministic certification cases and three independently seeded Monte Carlo populations of 1000 cases each. All cases satisfy the predefined first-zero error tolerance of 0.020, with no saturation or convergence failures. A stricter 0.1% accuracy threshold is reported for characterization but is not used as the certification criterion. We also demonstrate a dimensionless four-state radiative extension beyond polytropic models. The results establish a reproducible framework for analog computation of stellar-structure benchmarks while explicitly distinguishing ideal calculations, vendor-macromodel verification, and future physical-hardware validation.

 - **Authors:** János Takátsy, Henrique Gieg, Tim Dietrich
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30233

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30233

 - **Abstract**
 Strong first-order phase transitions in the cores of neutron stars (NSs) can have profound implications for astrophysical observables, however, their existence remains an open question. Since most NSs form in binary systems, they are expected to experience prolonged episodes of mass accretion that gradually increase their central density and can potentially trigger such a transition. This might, in turn, generate observable multi-messenger signals, as suggested by previous studies. Motivated by this prospect, we generate a new set of equations of state for hadron--quark matter with first-order phase transitions of various strengths, including neutrino absorption opacities in the quark phase as well. We then perform general-relativistic radiation hydrodynamics simulations of NSs undergoing accretion-induced phase transitions. In contrast to previous findings, our results indicate that these systems do not produce unbound ejecta for the models considered, making accretion-induced phase transitions unlikely sources of observable electromagnetic transients. However, we find that transitions are accompanied by intense neutrino emission, with peak luminosities reaching $\sim10^{52}~\mathrm{erg/s}$ for the strongest transition considered. To facilitate future numerical studies, we make the family of equations of state and neutrino opacities used in this work publicly available, enabling the systematic exploration of the role of the phase transition strength.

 - **Abstract**
 The early evolutionary stages of some active regions (ARs) are analysed with the aim of resolving one of the paramount-importance problems of heliophysics: what plays a primary, active role in the formation of active regions and sunspots---magnetic fields or motion of solar plasma? In particular, the first view underlies the popular model of rising $\Omega$-shaped, twisted flux-tube loop of strong magnetic field. In this study, optical images obtained by the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI) of the Solar Dynamics Observatory are used along with full-vector maps of magnetic field based on HMI data. Maps of fluid-particle tracks are constructed using local correlation tracking of photospheric brightness elements. It is concluded that plasma flows appear to be determinant with respect to magnetic fields. The considered scenarios of the origin of ARs are in sharp contradiction with the rising-tube model.

We test this assumption using the largest sample assembled for this purpose to date -- 9,328 Kepler red giants spanning the red giant branch and red clump phase, cross-matched with APOGEE stellar parameters. We model each star's power spectrum background to obtain the radius-corrected amplitude and characteristic frequency of both components, and derive empirical scaling relations with effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity through Bayesian model comparison. Metallicity raises both amplitudes but has little effect on the frequencies. Our results indicate that the self-similarity assumption underlying two-component background models does not hold, with direct implications for background modeling and its downstream application in Galactic archaeology.
