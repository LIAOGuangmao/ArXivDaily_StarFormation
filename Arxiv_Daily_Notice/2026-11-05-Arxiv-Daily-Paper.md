 - **Abstract**
 The emergence of gravitational wave (GW) multimessenger astronomy, marked by the detection of GW170817 and its associated kilonova, has opened a new window into the physics of compact binary mergers. Building a statistical population of kilonovae remains a challenge, limited by the low neutron star merger detection rates and the rapid evolution of these faint transients. The former problem will be overcome with upcoming LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA (LVK) sensitivity improvements leading to the fifth Observing Run (O5), while the latter will be addressed by the Schmidt Observatory System (including the Argus Array, the Deep Synoptic Array (DSA), and the Lazuli Space Observatory), in addition to ongoing and upcoming surveys (including the Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time). Finally, we develop an observing strategy that combines high cadence imaging and spectral monitoring, maximizing Lazuli's ability to constrain ejecta properties and composition of GW counterparts with reasonable observing time.

 - **Abstract**
 We study the evolution of self-gravitating spherical dark matter halos composed of dark matter particles that decay to a slightly less massive particle and a massless particle. The small kick velocity imparted to the daughter particle upon decay heats the halo. If the kick velocity is small compared with the characteristic velocity, the halo remains bound but puffs out. We also consider, for the first time, decaying halos with anisotropic initial conditions and find non-negligible effects on the resulting density profile. To simulate the halo evolution, we utilize the recently developed \texttt{NSphere} code across a broad range of decay times and kick velocities. We train an emulator to provide a mapping between the decay parameters and the relaxed density profile. We discuss how the resulting halo-scale signatures complement existing cosmological constraints on decaying dark matter and can be tested by upcoming surveys.

 We present measurements of the mass of the Milky Way using distant halo stars selected from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Data Release 2 (DR2). We apply four dynamical modeling methods to 10,036 RR Lyrae (RRL) and blue horizontal branch (BHB) stars with 6D phase space coordinates that span from $\sim$10 to 95 kpc from the Galactic center, the largest samples of their kind. We perform a deep MURaM small-scale dynamo simulation, extending from $50$~Mm below the solar surface and with a horizontal size of 60~Mm and sufficient resolution to begin to properly resolve the magnetic bright points in intergranular lanes.  - **Abstract**
 Ganymede is the only Solar System satellite with a global magnetic field driven by a core dynamo. Although its small moment-of-inertia (MoI) factor indicates a differentiated structure of a metallic core, rocky mantle, and outer hydrosphere, its exact bulk composition and layer thicknesses remain highly uncertain. We developed a comprehensive, long-term thermochemical evolution model that couples thermal history with magnetic field generation. Building upon previous theoretical frameworks, this study systematically evaluates how diverse chondritic precursor compositions constrain Ganymede's internal thermochemical evolution and core solidification processes. Based on interior structures initially permitted by a wide range of chondritic compositions and geodetic constraints, we applied the observed equatorial surface magnetic field strength ($\sim$719 nT) as a dynamic filter. Our evolutionary simulations demonstrate that while magnetic dynamos can operate under entire melting, Fe-snow, and FeS-layer regimes, the requirement to sustain the observed field strength strongly restricts the viable interior models.
