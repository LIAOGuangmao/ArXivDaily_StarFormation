#### Assessing the detectability of a realistic massive black hole binary population in LSST
 - **Authors:** Lorenzo Bertassi, Beatrice Sala, Edyn Davies, Fabio Rigamonti, Maria Charisi, Riccardo Buscicchio, Paola Severgnini, Massimo Dotti
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33159

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33159

 - **Abstract**
 Electromagnetic signatures of massive black hole binaries (MBHB) are expected to be found in quasar light curves. Specifically, MBHB light curves are expected to show periodic modulations which may originate from different physical processes. The detection of such periodicities requires algorithms suitable to model modulations with arbitrary shapes embedded in quasar red noise, in massive datasets. With the advent of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), such sources may finally be within reach in the next decade. In this work, we assess the performance of a recently proposed flexible detection algorithm for periodicities of arbitrary shapes with simulated LSST-like data that span its 10-year baseline. For the first time, we examine a physically motivated population of binaries and assess the effect of assumed priors on the period on the periodicity search. We study two methods to set the detection threshold: the first based on the posterior odds ratio, which assumes knowledge of the expected fraction of binaries in quasar light curves, and the second, which limits the detection of false positives. We find that the expected distribution of detected periods shows a clear peak between 5 and 10 years, depending on the specific LSST band considered. We recover a detected binary fraction ranging from ~ 20% to ~ 3% depending on the detection threshold assumed and the band analysed, with the peak of the observed period distribution depending on the underlying binary period distribution. Therefore, the detection of a population of binaries in LSST can constrain the binary population and shed light on their evolution.
#### Trapped sausage modes in magnetic pores: existence criteria and viscous damping
 - **Authors:** Zanyar Ebrahimi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Fluid Dynamics (physics.flu-dyn); Plasma Physics (physics.plasm-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33251

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33251

 - **Abstract**
 The conditions governing the existence and evolution of trapped sausage waves in photospheric magnetic pores are investigated, with particular emphasis on the limitations of analytical cut-off criteria and the role of viscous dissipation. A pore is modelled as a cylindrical magnetic flux tube with smoothly varying radial profiles of magnetic field and density, subject to the constraint that its temperature is lower than that of the surrounding atmosphere. For finite plasma-$\beta$, the linearised equations governing sausage-mode perturbations are derived, and an initial-value problem is solved numerically to obtain the full spatio-temporal evolution of the waves. The applicability of the analytical cut-off relation derived for a flux tube with step-function equilibrium profiles is examined, revealing that its predictions depend on the equilibrium parameters. The simulations reveal distinct wave behaviours across different parameter regimes, including the simultaneous excitation of trapped fast and slow sausage waves for a representative photospheric parameter set. Viscous damping is further investigated through the temporal evolution of the wave energy. Energy dissipation increases with decreasing viscous Reynolds number, increasing longitudinal wavenumber, and increasing radial magnetic-field contrast. These trends are consistent with enhanced viscous damping associated with increasingly fine spatial gradients generated through phase mixing. The findings highlight the importance of radial equilibrium structure in assessing sausage-wave trapping and demonstrate the sensitivity of wave-energy dissipation to the physical parameters of photospheric magnetic pores.

 The magnetic field is the most fundamental observable in solar physics, while solar flares are the most violent, rapid magnetic energy-release events in the solar atmosphere.  - **Abstract**
 Homologous coronal mass ejections (CMEs) are sequential eruptions from the same active region. They are of particular space weather interest because they can interact and coalesce into compound ejecta, potentially amplifying their geomagnetic impact. Yet the full chain from recurrent initiation to magnetic merging has not been captured self-consistently in a single global simulation. We present a three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic model that generates three homologous CMEs from a bipolar active region by recurrent photospheric shearing. All three eruptions follow the same initiation mechanism by tether-cutting reconnection within a recurrently formed sigmoid, while the later CMEs propagate significantly faster than the first. We demonstrate that this speed hierarchy arises from progressive coronal preconditioning; preceding eruptions evacuate the overlying corona, reducing both mass loading and ambient drag. The faster later CMEs catch up with their predecessors, and the compression between them forms inter-CME current sheets. Reconnection within these current sheets then progressively merges the interacting flux systems, completing the cannibalistic process. Synthetic coronagraph images reproduce the apparent coalescence of multiple white-light fronts. This simulation provides the first self-consistent global view of the birth, preconditioning, and cannibalistic interaction of homologous CMEs, bridging recurrent eruption initiation and inter-CME magnetic merging in the near-Sun corona.

 - **Abstract**
 Synchrotron polarization is a powerful tool for diagnosing magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, yet prior analyses are confined to the optically thin regime, neglecting the effects of synchrotron self-absorption (SSA). Here, we incorporate SSA into the radiative transfer of polarized synchrotron emission and employ 3D MHD turbulence simulations to investigate its impacts on polarization statistics.  - **Authors:** Finia P. Jost, André da Silva Schneider, Almudena Arcones, Achim Schwenk, Evan O'Connor, Gerard Navó, Yeunhwan Lim
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33879

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33879

 - **Abstract**
 We construct new finite-temperature, composition-dependent equation of state (EOS) tables for astrophysical simulations by implementing a recently developed energy-density functional. The functional is constrained by ab initio nuclear matter calculations and neutron-star observations. A distinguishing feature of this functional is a non-monotonic density dependence of the nucleon effective mass, with an increase beyond saturation density. Using these EOS tables, we investigate the impact of current uncertainties in nuclear matter properties on core-collapse supernova (CCSN) evolution with spherically symmetric simulations employing an effective treatment of convective mixing. The magnitude of the EOS effects depends on the progenitor compactness. Within current constraints, variations in incompressibility, symmetry energy, and high-density pressure produce only modest differences during the early post-bounce evolution. In contrast, the effective mass dependence has a much stronger influence on proto-neutron star contraction and the explosion dynamics in a stellar model with high core compactness. Its non-monotonic density dependence leads to a qualitatively different contraction history and can alter the explosion outcome compared to mean-field effective-mass behaviors. Therefore, among the nuclear matter properties considered, the density dependence of the nucleon effective masses has by far the largest impact on CCSN dynamics. This highlights the importance of realistic effective-mass parameterizations in nuclear EOSs used in astrophysical simulations.

 - **Abstract**

This allows for detailed comparisons between the gross evolution of this GRMHD simulation and existing "spreading disk" analytical theories. We show that the long-term features of this evolution are well described by General Relativistic Thin Disk Theory (GRTDT), which reproduces various aspects of the GRMHD evolution to within a factor $\sim 2$, despite the disk in our simulation not being remotely thin, showing dynamically dominant (MAD) magnetic fields, and launching a powerful wind.  - **Abstract**

We derive the empirical selection function of the SDSS-V Galactic Genesis survey, the largest Milky Way Mapper program targeting red giant stars for Galactic structure studies. Treating the Galactic Genesis sample as a subsample of its 2MASS parent catalog, we model the selection function as a function of sky position, apparent H magnitude, and G-H color using a Bayesian binomial estimator, computing both a mean and maximum a posteriori (MAP) version. #### The Impact of Flyby Interactions on the Rotation of Open Clusters
 - **Authors:** Kyle R. Tregoning, Jeff J. Andrews
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35805

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35805

 - **Abstract**
 The Gaia astrometric mission has provided the first evidence for the bulk rotation of open clusters. While this rotation may arise from residual spin inherited from a cluster's parent molecular cloud, it is unclear whether this explanation always suffices, as angular momentum is carried away by escaping stars on the timescale of two-body relaxation. In particular, the recent observance of significant rotation ($v_{\rm rot} = 0.81 \pm 0.06$ km s$^{-1}$) in the dynamically old ($\approx 4$ Gyr) open cluster M67 motivates a study of another potential source of spin for Solar neighborhood clusters: tidal torquing from gravitational encounters with passing giant molecular clouds (GMCs). We derive an analytic estimate for the change in angular momentum of a cluster due to an impulsive interaction with a passing GMC, and verify this expectation by performing $N$-body simulations over a range of encounter parameters using REBOUND. For highly energetic encounters, significant angular momentum can be transferred, but the stars that receive the most angular momentum are ejected, leaving the cluster remnant with negligible net rotation. Even when considering a random walk of many GMC-cluster encounters, the integrated effects of these interactions cannot explain observations of dynamically old rotating open clusters. However, we find that within $\sim 5$ Myr post-encounter, some disrupted clusters could be confused as rotating clusters while their stars are still dispersing. Using the local density of GMCs, we estimate that up to $\approx 1\%$ of open clusters in the Milky Way may carry this faux-rotation signal.

 - **Authors:** Navin Sridhar (Stanford University)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35849

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35849

 - **Abstract**
 The interaction of strong ($a_0>1$), superluminal X-mode electromagnetic waves with magnetized pair plasma falls into two regimes set by $\varepsilon_{\rm B}\equiv a_0\omega_{\rm B}/\omega_0$, the ratio of the wave strength parameter to its frequency, in units of cyclotron frequency. For $\varepsilon_{\rm B}<1$ the wave propagates until induced scattering disrupts it. Using analytical theory and kinetic simulations, we show that the background magnetic field introduces a restoring frequency $\Omega_{\rm B}$ to scattering; when $\Omega_{\rm B}$ exceeds the scattering rate, the unattenuated length grows from $\simeq\varepsilon_{\rm p}^{-2/3}$ to $\simeq\varepsilon_{\rm B}^{1/2}/\varepsilon_{\rm p}$ wavelengths ($\varepsilon_{\rm p}\equiv a_0\omega_{\rm p}/\omega_0$; $\omega_{\rm p}$ is the plasma frequency). When $a_0^2>\varepsilon_{\rm B}>1$, the plasma current is nonlinear, and the wave is damped while riding a motional electric field, so one half-cycle appears attenuated. This framework has implications for the duration of fast radio bursts and whether they can escape their surroundings.
