 - **Abstract**
 There are only three white dwarfs known to possess metal-enriched polar caps due to magnetically guided accretion. These rare systems offer a unique opportunity to study planetesimal accretion and how magnetic fields influence the spreading of material across the atmosphere. #### Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star Mergers
 - **Authors:** Ryan J. Foley
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40782

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40782

 - **Abstract**
 The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) collaboration has detected two binary neutron star (BNS) mergers, GW170817 and GW190425. While no clear electromagnetic counterpart was discovered for GW190425, a coincident counterpart to GW170817 (GRB170817A, AT 2017gfo) was discovered in an early-type galaxy, NGC 4993. Using broad-band photometry and an integrated optical spectrum, we measure a mass-weighted age of $12.4^{+0.5}_{-0.7}$ Gyr (90% credible) for NGC 4993, which is an estimate of the delay time between star formation and merger for the GW170817 progenitor. BNS systems are expected to have a power-law delay-time distribution (DTD) with a minimum delay time, $t_{\rm min}$, of ~10 Myr and index $\alpha \approx -1$. This canonical DTD, convolved with the cosmic star-formation history, predicts ~16 BNS mergers at delay times of ~100 Myr for every BNS merger at 10 Gyr; under it, the chance of detecting even one merger as old as GW170817 is ~1.8%. The long delay time of GW170817 therefore implies many "missing" shorter-delay mergers if the canonical DTD holds. A DTD with $t_{\rm min} = 12.6$ Gyr and $\alpha = -1$ is favored over the canonical DTD by a factor of 40-140, and over short gamma-ray burst and Galactic double-NS DTDs by factors of 80-280. These results are in tension with current population synthesis models, the Milky Way double NS population, and the SGRB population. Explaining the LVK population requires either a dramatically different separation distribution than commonly assumed, an additional channel with a long delay time and comparable rate to an isolated-binary formation scenario, or a large population of BNS mergers missed in the LVK analysis. This result has wide-ranging implications for binary-population synthesis, chemical enrichment models, connections between BNS mergers and SGRBs, and future gravitational wave observations.

 - **Abstract**
 We present a galaxy-scale black hole (BH) accretion and feedback model incorporating results from multi-zone general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations spanning the event horizon to the Bondi radius. The model replaces a fixed suppression of the Bondi rate with a nuclear-environment-dependent factor, $\kappa^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$, set by the local ratio of Bondi to gravitational radius, and distributes outflow mass, kinetic energy, and thermal energy according to the angular structure measured in the large-dynamic-range GRMHD calculations.  - **Abstract**
 Identifying the astrophysical sites of the heavy rapid neutron-capture process ($r$-process) remains one of the most prominent unsolved problems in nuclear astrophysics. Emitted $\gamma$-rays from the decay of unstable nuclei have been identified as a potential observable marker on both prompt and delayed timescales which could provide conclusive evidence of the presence of $r$-process activity.
