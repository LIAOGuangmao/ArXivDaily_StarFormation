#### Arepo-CD: Coupling Collisional $N$-Body Dynamics with Moving-Mesh Magnetohydrodynamics
 - **Authors:** Chuizheng Kong, Hui Li, Long Wang, Yunwei Deng, Elizabeth Moreno-Hilario
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28727

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28727

 - **Abstract**
 Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional $N$-body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.
#### Exploring the origin of slow-rotating pulsars: Emission from STARS simulated white dwarfs
 - **Authors:** Zenia Zuraiq, Banibrata Mukhopadhyay
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28728

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28728

 - **Abstract**
 An increasing number of slow-rotating pulsars and long-period transients have been observed in recent years. The periods of these sources can be significantly longer than typical pulsar sources, ranging from ~ 100 - 1000 s, or even longer. The observed periods and period derivatives predict high magnetic fields, higher than the Schwinger field, when we consider conventional pulsar models of dipole-driven emission from neutron stars. Interestingly, the periods of these sources are consistent with the current observations of white dwarfs (WDs). We explore the possibility of magnetic WDs, simulated using the stellar evolution code STARS, as the origin of some of these unusual pulsar observations. We simulate WDs with enhanced magnetic fields due to accretion after their formation. We also briefly touch on the more exotic possibility of super-Chandrasekhar WDs as the origin for these sources. We find that expanding the parameter space to include the possibility of a WD origin provides an alternate and viable explanation for these unusual observations.
#### Saturation Level of the Streaming Instability
 - **Authors:** Yanqin Wu, Xuqi Chen, Joshua Bromley (Toronto)

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28736

 - **Abstract**
 In proto-planetary disks, gas turbulence can cluster dust grains and catalyze the formation of planetesimals. This process depends on the strength of the turbulence. Here, we evaluate the turbulence strength that is induced by the streaming instability. Based on the amount of free energy available, we estimate the viscous parameter to be alpha ~ St Pi^2 f(epsilon), where St (< 1) is the Stokes number of the streaming grains, Pi is the dimensionless pressure gradient, and f (epsilon) is an explicit function of epsilon, the local dust-to-gas density ratio. This estimate explains the results of many previous studies, and is also supported by our additional tailor-made simulations. We discuss two easy applications of our result, one is a self-consistent dust scale height, one is the astrophysical relevance of a pressure bump. Lastly, while this work allows one to characterize the streaming turbulence, we emphasize our current inability to connect the strength of turbulence to the formation of planetesimals.

 - **Abstract**
 The source PSR J0901-4046 shows a very long rotational period, of $\approx 75.88$ s. In fact, its long period suggests that it could be a white dwarf. We examine the properties of the source and compare the outcomes under the hypotheses of the source being a highly magnetized white dwarf (B-WD) and neutron star.  - **Authors:** Frank C. van den Bosch, Yarone M. Tokayer

 - **Abstract**
 Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes undergo gravothermal evolution whose late-time behaviour is central to predictions for dense substructure and black-hole seed formation. We revisit the calibration of this evolution using a new spherical Direct Simulation Monte Carlo code, SphDSMC, which follows spherically symmetric SIDM systems without force softening and to substantially higher central densities than standard N-body simulations. We compare SphDSMC calculations with conventional N-body simulations and with one-dimensional gravothermal fluid models. We show that N-body results converge to SphDSMC only for softening lengths significantly smaller than those normally adopted for collisionless simulations, and that allowing multiple scatterings per particle per time step does not introduce significant errors when implemented appropriately. Calibrating the gravothermal fluid equations against SphDSMC, we find that the commonly used harmonic interpolation between the long- and short-mean-free-path conductivities is inaccurate. The revised calibration gives a long-mean-free-path collapse time that is somewhat shorter than previous estimates, but we show that collapse slows dramatically once the core becomes optically thick. We provide fitting functions for the late-time evolution and argue that black-hole seed formation by SIDM gravothermal collapse has generally been underestimated in duration, making it unlikely to explain the massive high-redshift black holes inferred from JWST observations without additional physics.

 - **Abstract**
 KROME is a Python pre-processor that writes a complete Fortran solver of chemistry and microphysics for each chemical network, widely used in astrophysical simulations. We introduce here KROME-X, a complete rewrite of KROME in modern C++, which incorporates updated physics. A code generator translates a declarative network description into a compact core (rates, right-hand side, analytic Jacobian, thermal terms, and tables), which a fixed, header-only runtime integrates with a C++ port of the `backward differentiation formula' method of CVODE (SUNDIALS). On the legacy test cases KROME-X reproduces legacy KROME to the accuracy of the solvers, wherever the physics is the same, and is up to 13.5 times faster at equal tolerance. On NVIDIA A100 GPUs, it can outperform its share of a computing node from a few thousand cells per call, with the highest throughput (5 to 30) obtained for $10^5$ cells per batch. Networks of hundreds of species still run faster on the CPU due to the GPU's limited memory capacity. The new version also includes, among other changes, on-the-fly co-evolution of a photon field alongside chemistry, which conserves photons exactly; the secondary ionizations using the photoelectron energy of each absorption; dust evolution in size and temperature with a true grain energy equation; line cooling rates rebuilt from current atomic and molecular data, and incorporating optical thickness effects. KROME-X therefore represents the natural successor of KROME and provides a portable, extensible framework for astrochemical kinetics on both CPUs and GPUs for future hydrodynamic simulations.

 - **Abstract**
 High-energy $\gamma$-ray emission has been detected from several star clusters, indicating that these sources are sites of cosmic-ray acceleration. However, it is still unclear whether the $\gamma$-ray emission is of hadronic or leptonic origin. We present a comprehensive study of the $\gamma$-ray emission from Westerlund 1, under the assumption that high-energy particles are accelerated at the cluster WTS. We investigate both the hadronic and leptonic scenarios by fitting simultaneously the spectrum and morphology of the $\gamma$-ray emission. We numerically solve the transport equations for non-thermal protons and electrons for a given $K_\rm{ep}$ and derive their energy and spatial distributions. $\gamma$ rays are produced either by hadronic pp interactions of protons with the target gas or by inverse Compton scattering of relativistic electrons (both primary and secondary) with the ambient photon fields. For reasonable model parameters, we find that in the case of Bohm diffusion, both the leptonic and hadronic scenarios can account for the observed multi-wavelength spectrum and $\gamma$-ray morphology. For the leptonic scenario, the magnetic field immediately downstream of the shock can be constrained to be $\lesssim$ a few $\mu$G thanks to the eROSITA upper limit. On the other hand, in order to explain the $\gamma$-ray observations with hadronic emission alone, the gas density inside the bubble should be $\gtrsim 1.2 \, \rm{cm}^{-3}$.
