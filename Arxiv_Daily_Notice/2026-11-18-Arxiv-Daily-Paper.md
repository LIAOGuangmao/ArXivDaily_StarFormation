#### Shocks and Trails in the Circumgalactic Medium Driven by Runaway Supermassive Black Holes
 - **Authors:** Kung-Yi Su, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21738

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21738

 - **Abstract**
 Runaway supermassive black holes (SMBHs) have long been predicted. RBH-1, discovered with HST and confirmed by JWST, provides the strongest evidence yet for a runaway SMBH: a bow shock and linear trail in the circumgalactic medium (CGM) of a $z=0.96$ galaxy. The bow shock is $\sim$kpc wide, the trail is $\sim 60$ kpc long, and the SMBH apparently moves at $\sim 1,000$ km s$^{-1}$. Using controlled hydrodynamic simulations, we show that the SMBH's gravity alone cannot produce a significant bow shock, but a comoving wind source can sustain one similar to RBH-1's. The wind must either be active or have shut off less than $\sim 3$ Myr ago. The required recent wind momentum flux is $6\times10^{32}\,{\rm dyn}\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$, where $n_{\rm CGM}$ is the CGM density. Assuming the wind reaches peak strength only during the last $\sim 6$ Myr, the minimum integrated wind mass is $3.5\times10^5\,M_\odot\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$. A $2\times10^7\,M_\odot$ SMBH accreting at $1\%$ Eddington could power the wind. Supernova feedback from a star cluster carried by the SMBH is an alternative, although the required mass and momentum fluxes $\gtrsim 60$ Myr after ejection are difficult to reconcile with a standard initial mass function. For a hot ambient CGM at $\sim 10^6$ K, the quasi-linear trail constrains the 1D velocity dispersion to $\sigma_{\rm 1D}\lesssim 30$ km s$^{-1}$, above which it becomes strongly disturbed. Runaway SMBH trails therefore offer a new probe of turbulence in the diffuse CGM, in regimes where its kinematics are otherwise inaccessible.

 - **Abstract**
 We present multi-wavelength observations and modeling of the largest sample to date of core-collapse supernovae (SNe) with Hubble Space Telescope (HST) ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy within < 2 weeks of first light. Our total sample contains early-time UV spectroscopy from 12 Type II SNe (SNe II), 3 Type IIb SNe (SNe IIb), 3 Type IIn SNe (SNe IIn) and 1 Type I super-luminous SN (SLSN-I). This sample includes 11 objects with unpublished UV spectroscopy from HST Cycles 31 and 32 "Flexible Thursdays" target of opportunity programs, increasing the number of core-collapse SNe with early-time HST UV spectra by a factor of 2.4. The early-time near-UV spectra of most sample SNe II show similar Doppler-broadened P-Cygni profiles of metals (Mg, Fe, Ti, Cr), though with notable diversity in line velocities and absorption strengths. Comparison to non-Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (nLTE) radiative transfer model grids at similar phases indicates that most sample SNe II are consistent with low circumstellar material (CSM) densities at the time of UV observations (e.g., $\dot M = 10^{-6}$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, $v_w = 50$ km/s) and standard kinetic energies (e.g., $E_k = 0.6-1.2$ B), while two objects, SNe 2024faq and 2023ixf, show UV/optical evidence for interaction with more confined, higher-density CSM. SN 2023ixf and sample SNe IIn exhibit relatively featureless early-time UV spectra despite displaying prominent optical Lorentzian emission lines from electron scattering in optically thick CSM -- this being attributed to differing properties of UV resonance versus optical recombination transitions as well as intrinsic CSM asymmetries. These results demonstrate the potential of early-time UV spectroscopy to constrain the outermost SN ejecta structure, explosion energetics/composition, and progenitor mass-loss histories of core-collapse SNe.

 - **Abstract**
 Recurring flux emergence within active nests reshapes the Sun's large-scale magnetic field and creates unipolar magnetic regions that can support coronal holes. The fast solar wind emerging from coronal holes drives space weather across the heliosphere. We investigated how the emergence of active nests at the peak of solar cycle 25 drove changes in global magnetic topology, coronal hole formation, and solar wind speed. Coronal holes were identified from composite SDO/AIA maps and their magnetic field properties were extracted from SDO/HMI magnetograms. Spherical harmonic decomposition quantified how magnetic flux was distributed at different spatial scales and was used to extrapolate the coronal field. We compared coronal structure with SOHO/LASCO-C2 observations and the solar wind measured at 1 au. Emerging magnetic flux within active nests rapidly surpassed the pre-existing magnetic flux in 2024, strengthening the non-axisymmetric field and shifting the corona into a tilted-dipole state. This dipolarisation stabilised the large-scale magnetic field, with the heliospheric current sheet anchored above active nests. Unipolar magnetic regions from the edges of active nests sustained non-axisymmetric coronal holes covering 6 to 14% of the solar surface in 2025, comparable to the 12% covered by polar coronal holes at solar minimum. Flux emergence near the tilted-dipole's negative pole returned the corona to a quadrupole-dominated state in 2026. Active nests helped stabilise the coronal magnetic field, while the decay and transport of unipolar magnetic flux sustained persistent, non-axisymmetric coronal holes. Differential rotation between unipolar regions triggered episodic expansion and contraction of the largest coronal holes. Early identification of specific active nest configurations, combined with the solar cycle phase, could improve the forecasting of coronal holes and fast solar wind.

 - **Authors:** Tianyi Ding, Jose Alonso Carpio, Ali Kheirandish, Kohta Murase
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21752

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21752

 - **Abstract**
 The joint detection of high-energy neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays offers a powerful probe of the most energetic astrophysical accelerators, linking hadronic interactions to observable signatures across cosmic distances. Understanding the conditions under which these two messengers can be simultaneously observed is essential for identifying and characterizing the sources of cosmic neutrinos. In this work, we examine the detectability of $\gamma$-ray cascades associated with neutrino production in source environments that are gamma-ray transparent, focusing on how electromagnetic cascades evolve as $\gamma$-rays propagate through intergalactic space. By modeling photon interactions with the extragalactic background light and deflections in intergalactic magnetic fields, we determine the minimum neutrino fluence for transient emission and neutrino flux for steady emission required to produce a $\gamma$-ray counterpart detectable by $\gamma$-ray observatories, such as Fermi-LAT, HAWC, or LHAASO. This defines a ``multimessenger horizon" -- the region in flux-distance parameter space where neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays are jointly observable. We consider both energy-binned injection tied to IceCube's sensitivity and a continuous power-law spectrum extending from TeV to PeV energies. Our results reveal that detectability depends strongly on redshift, magnetic field strength, and photon energy, with higher-energy cascades suffering greater attenuation inside filamentary structures. The framework provides new constraints on source opacity, baryon loading, and neutrino production mechanisms, offering a predictive tool for interpreting current events and guiding future multimessenger campaigns.

 - **Abstract**
 Very massive stars above the pair-instability mass gap may collapse directly into black holes surrounded by massive disks. These "direct-horizon collapsars" accrete at $\dot{M} \gtrsim 10\,M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and have been proposed to eject a substantial amount of $r$-process material, powering "super-kilonovae". We present the first three-dimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of direct-horizon collapsars with M1 neutrino transport, following a $\approx 150\,M_\odot$ helium core from collapse through the escape of magnetically driven outflows. Extreme accretion rates neutronize the disks to $Y_{\rm e} \approx 0.1$-$0.25$ and, at early times, allow them to satisfy the gravitational-instability and cooling criteria for fragmentation. However, the strong poloidal fields needed to eject the neutron-rich gas promptly also drain the disks and lift their electron degeneracy. The neutron-rich ejecta composition is set in a brief phase, such that the ejecta carry $\sim 0.1$-$0.3\,M_\odot$ of nuclei with $A \geq 69$, including $\lesssim 2\times10^{-4}\,M_\odot$ of lanthanides, alongside $\sim 1$-$4\,M_\odot$ of $^{56}$Ni. Assuming proportional retention and mixing of Eu and Fe, these yields imply $[{\rm Eu/Fe}]\approx-0.4$ to $+0.3$, and simple dilution estimates permit enrichment at $[{\rm Fe/H}]\lesssim-2.6$. Direct-horizon collapsars are therefore potential prompt contributors to iron and $r$-process enrichment at low metallicity. Their predicted Eu/Fe ratios fall below those of strongly $r$-process-enhanced stars, but uncertainties in the relative yields, particularly of iron, may close this gap. The ${}^{56}{\rm Ni}$-rich ejecta would power luminous supernovae with rise times of weeks, potentially accompanied by exceptionally powerful gamma-ray bursts, rather than lanthanide-rich super-kilonovae.

 Blazars exhibit strong variability across the electromagnetic spectrum, on timescales from minutes to decades. We present a study of the spectral and timing variability of the TeV blazar PKS~2155$-$304 using {\it Chandra} and {\it NuSTAR} observations spanning 25 years (1999$-$2024). Timing analysis reveals fractional variability amplitudes of up to $F_{\rm var}\approx 24\%$ in the hard X-ray band and $F_{\rm var}\lesssim 7.5\%$ in the soft X-ray band, along with variable power-spectral indices across observations. Our results reveal energy-dependent X-ray variability over more than two decades, and show that broadband X-ray coverage is needed to locate the curvature of the synchrotron tail in high-synchrotron-peaked blazars.

 - **Abstract**
 Context. At low accretion rates, X-ray pulsars can develop a two-hump spectrum through the thermalization and Comptonization of cyclotron photons in an accretion-heated, strongly magnetized atmosphere.  - **Abstract**
 Omega Centauri (NGC 5139, $\omega$ Cen) is the most massive globular cluster in the Milky Way and the likely stripped nucleus of an accreted dwarf galaxy. Extensive HST observations have uncovered many of its peculiar features, including complex subpopulations and kinematic evidence for an intermediate-mass black hole via central fast-moving stars. High-precision astrometric follow-up is essential to constrain the orbits of these stars. We present oMEGACat-JWST, a deep photometric and astrometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core and surroundings that extends our previous oMEGACat efforts to near-infrared wavelengths. The superior sensitivity and angular resolution of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) provide significant astrometric and photometric improvements, particularly for faint stars. Our catalog is based on a custom reduction of NIRCam and NIRISS imaging across three JWST programs extending out to two half-light radii (10'). In total, our catalog contains approximately 677 000 sources. In the region overlapping with oMEGACat, we achieve high relative completeness (> 90% of HST sources recovered) while detecting over 86 000 new sources below HST's detection limit. Astrometric tests demonstrate a single-image precision as good as 0.5% of a NIRCam pixel (0.15 mas) at mF115W=18; at mF115W=23 the precision is still 1 mas. Color-magnitude diagrams based on our data show the well-known split between the primordial Helium and the Helium enriched populations and are particularly sensitive to light-element (CNO) variations of faint stars. oMEGACat-JWST provides the most complete photometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core to date. Combined with HST data, it enables multi-wavelength subpopulation analyses. Together with future monitoring observations, its high astrometric precision will improve our understanding of the kinematics of the central fast-moving stars in $\omega$ Cen.
