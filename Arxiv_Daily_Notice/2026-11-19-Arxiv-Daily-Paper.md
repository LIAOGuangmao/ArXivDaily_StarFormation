 - **Abstract**
 We present the first spatially resolved study of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) evolution in the galactic superwind of the nearby (d~3.5Mpc) galaxy NGC 253. Using JWST MIRI and NIRCam, we image ~$\pm$2kpc along the galaxy minor axis, capturing both sides of the starburst-driven wind at ~0.05''-0.375'' (~0.85-6.4pc) resolution and providing one of the most detailed views of the wind to date. We target PAH emission at 3.3um, 7.7um, and 11.3um, revealing PAH-bright filamentary streamers along the limb-brightened edges of the outflow cones out to at least a deprojected distance r~0.5kpc, beyond which they likely become confused with the spiral arms. In the southwest (SW) and northeast (NE) streamers, PAH intensity declines faster than $r^{-2}$, suggesting either PAH destruction or a decrease in gas column density or dust-to-gas ratio rather than a response to declining illumination alone. Band ratios trace changes in PAH properties, with the 11.3/7.7um ratio increasing with distance in the SW and northwest (NW) streamers and 3.3/7.7um and 3.3/11.3um decreasing in the SW and NE streamers, indicating larger, more neutral PAHs farther from the starburst. Given the thermal and dynamical properties of the gas in the SW streamer, cool gas is expected to survive acceleration in the outflow and enable PAH shielding. Compared with those of the prototypical starburst M82, the NGC 253 band ratios indicate faster evolution in PAH size. Thus, the NGC 253 wind may represent an earlier stage of outflow evolution in which small PAHs are destroyed near the starburst within the first few Myr.

 - **Abstract**
 Fast X-ray transients (FXTs) are extragalactic X-ray flashes with little or no accompanying $\gamma$-ray emission. We propose that FXTs and classical gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) share a common origin in Poynting-flux-dominated jets powered by the Blandford--Znajek mechanism. In this picture, FXTs arise when the kink instability triggers premature dissipation at small radii ($\sim 10^{11}$~cm), yielding low Lorentz factors ($\Gamma \lesssim 50$) and high compactness ($\ell' \gg 1$) that thermalize the emission into the soft X-ray band. When the kink criterion is not met, the jet accelerates to ultra-relativistic velocities ($\Gamma > 100$) and dissipates at larger radii ($\sim 10^{15}$~cm) as a standard GRB. This scenario can naturally explain the observed thermal spectra, low $E_{\rm peak}$, supernova associations, jet-break absence, and day-scale afterglow delays across five well-studied FXTs. EP240801a is explained as a boundary event, where premature dissipation does not exhaust the full magnetic energy; the residual energy is further dissipated at a typical GRB radius, producing the observed $\gamma$-ray prompt emission with an $\sim 80$~s time lag. A key observational prediction is that FXTs may exhibit $e^+ e^-$ annihilation line features sweeping from a few MeV to hundreds of keV within $\sim 0.01$~s.

 - **Abstract**
 We consider how to distribute the magnetic flux on a star's surface in order to maximise the proportion of open magnetic flux predicted by the potential field source surface model. The proportion of open flux is shown to be maximised by concentrating all of the positive and negative flux at antipodal points. For a typical source surface height, this enhances the open flux by 35% compared to a dipolar distribution.  We report the discovery of a GeV $\gamma$-ray source spatially offset from SNR G21.8-3.0, while no significant high-energy emission is detected from the remnant itself.  In accreting neutron stars, different heating depths can produce similar quiescent cooling when the heating strength and core temperature are adjusted.
