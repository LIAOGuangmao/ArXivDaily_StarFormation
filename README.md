 - **Authors:** Levi Schult, Alexander Criswell, Stephen R. Taylor, Katelyn Breivik
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17421

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17421

 - **Abstract**
 Electromagnetic observations have been the primary medium for studying cataclysmic variable (CV) systems, capitalizing on the invaluable opportunity they present to study binary stellar evolution and accretion. The launch of the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) mission, however, will enable a multi-messenger perspective of the population. We extend previous work to explore the gravitational wave (GW) spectra sourced by a local and Galactic population of CVs. Using the Bayesian LISA Inference Package (BLIP), we simulate LISA data containing these CV populations to understand their impact and our ability to jointly model CV and double white dwarf (DWD) GW spectra. Current approaches struggle to accurately recover both populations' respective spectra, calling for the creation of multi-component spectral models that include GW contributions from both CVs and DWDs. These results point toward a promising future for the study of CVs with LISA.

 - **Abstract**
 The central compact object (CCO) 1E 1207.4-5209 is remarkable in that its X-ray spectrum shows clear absorption features at ~0.7 and 1.4 keV. Attributing these features to electron cyclotron resonance, the implied surface magnetic field is B~(7-8)x10^10 G, which agrees approximately with the field inferred from the spin properties of the pulsar. Here we examine all XMM-Newton EPIC pn spectra from more than 30 observations of 1E 1207.4-5209 from 2001-2020.  Context. Low-ionization structures (LISs) are small, dense clumps within planetary nebulae (PNe) that stand out in low-ionization emission lines, but the physical processes responsible for their excitation and the nature of H2 remain poorly understood.  - **Authors:** Casey McGrath

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17571

 - **Abstract**
 Gravitational-wave (GW) radial velocity (RV) may offer a new method for detecting exoplanets, one that would primarily target planets orbiting GW-emitting, ultracompact, post-main-sequence stellar objects such as white dwarfs, neutron stars, and black holes. In this work we show that while similar to electromagnetic-based RV, this GW-based method depends on the ''phase modulation index'' (PMI), a parameter that is more sensitive to high-mass, long-period exoplanets. We investigate the potential of GW RV for GW sources in both the mid-frequency spectrum (where the upcoming LISA mission will operate) and the high-frequency spectrum (where the current LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) observatories operate). By analyzing the power content of the observed GW signal, we derive analytic expressions to predict the GW carrier frequency power loss due to the exoplanet and the signal-to-noise ratio of the GW source and the exoplanet. We also explore the effects of the additional Doppler effect caused by the motion of the GW observatory and the potential for GW source parameter bias introduced by the presence of an undetected exoplanet. All of this helps us better understand where the GW RV method may work best in the exoplanet mass-period discovery space.

#### Observations of stellar feedback: tracers, diagnostics, measurements, and physical interpretation
 - **Authors:** Anna F. McLeod
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17592

 - **Abstract**
 Stellar feedback is central to galaxy evolution, yet the coupling of radiation, stellar winds, and supernovae to the multi-phase interstellar medium remains uncertain. This review treats this as a problem of inference, framed around four qualitative axes of ignorance: observational diagnostics are indirect; stellar population synthesis models must be ground-truthed before being used to infer feedback quantities; recent high-redshift observations have opened a new regime that may require re-calibration; and stellar feedback and the affected environment are interdependent. We examine the inference chain, from observables to physical quantities and from those quantities to how energy and momentum are transferred to the surrounding medium, across the principal diagnostics of stellar feedback: ionised gas, mechanically-driven bubbles, giant molecular cloud lifetimes, galactic outflows, and Lyman-continuum escape. We ask how much observations reveal about the link between the feedback-driving massive stars and the feedback-driven interstellar medium. We further discuss how stellar population synthesis assumptions, environment (e.g., metallicity, dust content), and galaxy properties (e.g., stellar mass, gas content) affect feedback measurements, and how high-redshift observations are testing calibrations established in the nearby Universe. The emerging picture is that interpreting a feedback measurement requires explicit control of the inference chain and of its physical context; feedback should therefore be contextualised, i.e. interpreted in terms of mechanism, gas phase, environment, and spatial and temporal scale. Progress will depend on measuring feedback-related quantities across the full range of star-forming systems and, more importantly, on obtaining observations (real, and synthetic from post-processed simulations) that match the same tracer and spatial scale across the same systems.
#### Polarization of strongly lensed gravitational waves as a probe of the lens-induced orientations of electromagnetic counterparts
 - **Authors:** Kyungmin Kim
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17637

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17637

 - **Abstract**
 Strong gravitational lensing is a well-established phenomenon in electromagnetic (EM) observations, where the lensed images and arcs are resolved on the two-dimensional sky and constrain the mass distribution of the lens. Strongly lensed gravitational waves (GWs) are also expected to be detected routinely by next-generation detectors, but their multiple images are recorded only as one-dimensional time series without positions. Among them, lensed binary neutron star mergers can be accompanied by EM counterparts with resolvable structures, such as relativistic jets. In EM lensing, the lens-induced change in the apparent orientation of such a structure can be measured only relative to other images or statistically, because its intrinsic orientation is not directly observable. In this work, we show that the GW polarization, which the lens leaves unchanged, provides this missing reference, i.e., a compass, so that the orientation offset of each image can be measured in absolute terms. We place this orientation layer on the EM image plane together with image positions from the lensed host arcs and Fermat-potential heights from GW time delays and magnifications. Using a mock population of such mergers observable by the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer, we find that the predicted image positions and arrival times are calibrated once the detection selection is included. The lens-induced orientation offsets reach tens of degrees and are detectable in up to a third of the events, at viewing angles above about $40^\circ$ for quadrupole-dominated signals.

 - **Abstract**
 Recent white-light observations from the Metis coronagraph on board Solar Orbiter have shown the presence of propagating density fluctuations with a period of $\sim5$ minutes, particularly in the observationally bright regions of helmet streamers and pseudo-streamers. These density fluctuations have been shown to originate from magnetohydrodynamic wave modes -- slow and fast modes can directly compress the plasma, whereas Alfvén waves generate density fluctuations via a non-linear coupling with the slow and fast modes.
