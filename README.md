#### line racer: Rapid Calculation of Exoplanetary Radiative Opacities
 - **Authors:** David B. Hägele, Paul Mollière, Michiel Min
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33290

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33290

 - **Abstract**
 Detailed studies of exoplanet and brown dwarf atmospheres rely on precise knowledge of the spectral features of possible atmospheric species. These features are a result of the interaction of different molecules and atoms with the radiation of the host star or the intrinsic thermal radiation of the object. The interaction is described by the opacity of a species, which determines how much light is absorbed at a given wavelength. The strength and shape of these opacities are very dependent on the temperature and pressure of the atmosphere, which is why they have to be calculated for a wide range of conditions. line racer is a Python package that enables computing high-resolution opacities from large molecular and atomic line lists in an effective manner. It offers users a wide range of options to customize opacity calculations to their needs and available hardware. The code is designed for efficient parallelization on multi-core and multi-node systems and can produce outputs compatible with popular atmospheric modeling and retrieval tools.
#### The fate of Mercury's ejected mantle
 - **Authors:** Tuhin Ghosh, Mark Wyatt, Alan P. Jackson, Oliver Shorttle, Maximilian Sommer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33297

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33297

 - **Abstract**
 Mercury's unusually high core mass fraction, inferred from its high bulk density, remains difficult to explain. Mantle stripping by a giant impact provides a plausible origin, but without efficient removal, the ejected debris remains on Mercury-crossing orbits and later gets re-accreted. Here, we show that re-accretion of impact debris onto Mercury is inefficient, making a giant-impact origin plausible. We combine N-body simulations of giant-impact ejecta with a collisional evolution model and consideration of processes acting on small dust. We find that, when the ejecta are dominated by large, several hundred km-sized non-vaporized fragments, the debris disk remains optically thin and the collisional cascade is truncated near the radiation-pressure blowout limit, where micron-sized grains are removed. In contrast, if vaporized, the ejecta recondense into mm to cm-sized droplets and form an optically thick disk, in which radiation pressure cannot efficiently truncate the cascade. The debris can then be ground down to nanometre-sized grains, where collisions become predominantly non-fragmenting and dissipative rather than destructive. Subsequent inelastic collisions damp the eccentricities and inclinations of the nanodust, producing a geometrically thin disk whose lifetime is governed by removal through exposed surface layers, where grains acquire large charge-to-mass ratios through solar UV radiation and are rapidly removed by electromagnetic forces. In either scenario, collisional evolution strongly reduces the re-accretion of debris and alleviates a key difficulty for the giant-impact origin of Mercury's large core. More broadly, highly energetic giant impacts in the inner regions of planetary systems may lead to short-lived populations of nanometre-sized dust.

 - **Abstract**
 GD 492 (LP 40-365) is a partly burnt, hypervelocity runaway stellar remnant thought to have survived a thermonuclear supernova. We analyze 12 sectors of TESS photometry spanning more than four years to investigate the previously reported 8.9-hr photometric modulation of the star. We find that the modulation changes with time, with individual-sector fits yielding apparent periods ranging from 8.8606 to 8.9048 hr (0.5% changes), more than 25 times the formal measurement uncertainties. Both the period and shape of the modulation evolve on a timescales of approximately one to two months. We show qualitatively that a two-spot model with evolving surface structure can reproduce several of the observed light-curve morphologies, although the geometry of the spots is not uniquely constrained. These observations suggest that changing surface inhomogeneities are a plausible origin of the photometric variability. Differential rotation and magnetic activity may contribute to this evolution, potentially providing new constraints on the outer convective structure of GD 492 and on the interpretation of its atmospheric abundances.

Observations suggest that these inflated radii can persist for Gyr timescales, even for planets receiving relatively low stellar irradiation. The physical mechanisms responsible for maintaining such large radii, however, remain poorly understood. Here, we model the thermal evolution of super-puffs with substantial H/He envelopes using a one-dimensional planetary structure and cooling model. We follow the coupled cooling and contraction of the planetary interior and H/He envelope while self-consistently calculating the planet's hydrostatic and radiative--convective structure.  - **Authors:** Kevin Tao, Thomas Loredo, David Ruppert

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34786

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34786

 - **Abstract**
 We describe a framework for building low-rank separable expansions to model spectroscopic data from systems with time-varying spectra, in settings where the spectral dimension is finely sampled (in wavelength), but the temporal dimension is sparsely and possibly irregulary sampled. The motivating application is detection and characterization of small exoplanets, e.g., Earth-like planets around Sun-like stars. Such planets cannot be seen directly, but can be detected via the minute Doppler shifts of the host star spectrum due to the star's motion as the planet and star revolve around their mutual center of mass. Current instruments are reaching the precision necessary to detect the ~10 cm/s motions corresponding to an exo-Earth. But the small Doppler shifts from such low-amplitude orbital motion can be masked or mimicked by the effects of stellar activity on the surface of the host star. e.g., starspots (dark regions), faculae (bright filaments), etc. One technique astronomers have used to try to model the stellar activity component of the signal is PCA, applied to an ensemble of spectra collected over time. But PCA neglects the continuous function nature of a spectrum at a given time, and the continuous evolution of spectra over time. We address model building from a function approximation perspective, via the asymmetric Hilbert-Schmidt decomposition (HSD). This provides a PCA-like low-rank decomposition of a dynamic spectrum, but it explicitly accounts for time dependence, both estimating temporal basis functions, and accounting for irregular temporal sampling when estimating spectral basis functions. #### A comparison of two- and one-fluid dust solvers for dusty, supersonic turbulence
 - **Authors:** Narges Vadood, Terrence S. Tricco
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34910

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34910

 - **Abstract**
 We model 3 and 10 $\mu$m dust grains in supersonic, turbulent molecular cloud conditions to compare two different numerical methods solving dust coupled to gas through a drag term. One method models dust and gas as two separate species (two fluid) and the other the combination of dust and gas as a single mixture (one fluid) using the diffusion approximation. Simulations are performed in a 3D periodic box using the Phantom code. The gas probability distributions are consistent with a lognormal distribution for both methods and grain sizes. The dust distributions are different in the two methods, showing discrepancies particularly in regions of low density. The most significant difference between the two methods is in the dust-to-gas ratio distributions. Both methods peak at the mean dust-to-gas ratio of 0.01, but the two-fluid method has wider distributions than the one-fluid method suggesting excess dust concentration in dense filaments. Filaments are where the one-fluid method results are most accurate. The narrowing of the one-fluid distribution is also caused by the stopping time limiter used to maintain the terminal velocity approximation. This artificially makes the mixture more coupled in low-density regions. Our overall conclusion is that both methods are viable for the study of dust in molecular clouds, but that the correct method should be chosen based on the numerical Stokes number regime of the calculation to avoid numerical artifacts.
