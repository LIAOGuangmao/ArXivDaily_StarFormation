#### Scalar-Assisted Growth of Supermassive Black Holes: Boson-Mass Windows and Observational Constraints
 - **Authors:** Jorge Castelo Mourelle, Darío Núñez Zúñiga, Saeed Fakhry, Nicolas Sanchis-Gual
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18986

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18986

 - **Abstract**
 We study a phenomenological cosmological model of black-hole growth in which a finite baryonic reservoir and a bosonic scalar cloud feed the black hole simultaneously. The baryonic component accretes through an Eddington-like channel, while the scalar cloud transfers mass through an effective quasibound-state decay rate. Evolving the system directly in redshift within a flat $\Lambda$CDM background, we scan over $\mu$, $M_0$, $M_{c,0}$, and $M_{b,0}$ to assess whether scalar-assisted accretion can accelerate the formation of $10^9M_\odot$ supermassive black holes. We find that the scalar contribution is highly selective in parameter space. For the mass scales explored here, scalar transfer is most efficient for $\mu\sim10^{-19}-10^{-16} \mathrm{eV}$, producing rapid cloud depletion and significantly earlier black-hole growth, while outside this interval the evolution remains mainly baryonic. More generally, this efficient window reflects a preferred range of the dimensionless coupling $\alpha=\mu M_{\rm BH}$ and therefore shifts approximately as $\mu\propto M_{\rm BH}^{-1}$. Comparison with observed quasar masses and luminosities instead favors lower boson masses, broadly around $\mu\sim10^{-21}-10^{-20}\mathrm{eV}$. These results are not necessarily in tension, since the boson mass that maximizes early growth depends on the characteristic black-hole mass and evolutionary stage. This separation between dynamical efficiency and observational compatibility leaves open complementary roles for different bosonic mass scales in supermassive black hole growth and dark-matter phenomenology.

 - **Abstract**
 We used a combination of James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations to investigate the mass functions (MFs) of the different stellar populations in the globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae (NGC 104) over a wide stellar-mass interval. We identified the multiple populations along the lower main sequence using only JWST photometry and, in particular, colour combinations sensitive to variations in oxygen abundance.  - **Abstract**
 On April 21, 2023, a complex filament eruption produced an Earth-directed coronal mass ejection (CME) that triggered the first severe geomagnetic storm of solar cycle 25. The primary objective of this work is to understand the physical origin of the severe impact of this CME on Earth. We explore the CME's complex morphology by simulating the eruption using the thermodynamic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) model CORHEL-CME. We model the pre-eruptive structure as two intertwined magnetic flux ropes and validate the early evolution of the event by comparing synthetic observables generated from the model with extreme-ultraviolet and white-light observations. We then compare the simulated CME structure with in situ measurements. From our analysis, we conclude that the CME merged with a stream interaction region (SIR) before reaching Earth. This interaction compressed the CME sheath, producing a sustained negative $B_n$ component that, together with the associated enhancement in solar wind density and velocity, triggered the geomagnetic storm. The internal configuration of the magnetic flux rope, which also maintained a sustained southward component during its propagation toward Earth, intensified the storm to severe levels. This model-data comparison underscores the power of using MHD simulations, when validated by multi-viewpoint observations, to reveal hidden dynamical processes and improve the understanding and prediction of space weather impacts.

 Obtaining accurate estimates of the gas column density $\mathrm{N(H_2)}$ is crucial to determine the physical conditions of the molecular gas in the Interstellar Medium. This paper introduces the "Multi-Tracer Column Density Synthesis" (MT-CDS) technique, a new empirical approach to derive high dynamic-range column density maps with interferometers such as ALMA. We have compared the integrated intensity maps of two density selective tracers, namely HNC (1-0) and $\mathrm{N_2H^+}$ (1-0), obtained with IRAM-30m with ancillary Herschel column density estimates in seven star-forming regions in Orion and derived individual temperature-corrected conversion factors for each of these tracers.  - **Abstract**
 Using three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic simulations accounting for ohmic dissipation, we investigate the long-term evolution of the infalling envelope for $\sim 100,000$\,yr after protostar formation. The primary control parameter is the initial misalignment angle $\theta_0$ between the rotation axis and the magnetic field, varied from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$. In all misaligned models ($\theta_0 \ne 0^\circ$), the pseudodisk (or infalling envelope) surrounding the circumstellar disk develops a highly distorted and complex morphology. Gas accretion from the envelope proceeds through discrete, channel-like flows, producing asymmetric streams rather than axisymmetric infall. Protostellar outflows are launched even in these complex environments, and inflow and outflow regions are strongly interwoven. Outflows transport angular momentum anisotropically, leading to time-dependent changes in disk orientation and outflow direction, which further enhance envelope asymmetry. Asymmetric accretion and stream-like structures persist beyond 10,000\,au, demonstrating that large-scale asymmetry can arise intrinsically. Our results show that complex streams and asymmetric envelopes naturally emerge from magnetized core physics, even in the absence of initial turbulence or external gas accretion.

 - **Abstract**
 While magnetic flux ropes (MFRs) are widely accepted to exist prior to many solar eruptions, the physical mechanism responsible for triggering and subsequently driving their eruptions remains debated. Classical models commonly attribute eruption onset to ideal magnetohydrodynamic instabilities, particularly the torus instability, while magnetic reconnection is often regarded as a secondary process. Using high-resolution three-dimensional MHD simulations, we investigate the eruption of an MFR that forms self-consistently through flux cancellation driven by converging motions and photospheric diffusion following an initial shearing phase. The pre-eruption MFR rises quasi-statically and reaches a height where the decay index exceeds the classical torus instability threshold, yet the system continues to evolve quasi-statically without eruption. Instead, eruption begins only when the current sheet beneath the MFR becomes sufficiently thin for magnetic reconnection to set in. Increasing numerical resolution systematically delays the eruption onset, whereas the decay index at eruption onset remains nearly unchanged, demonstrating that the onset is governed by reconnection rather than torus instability. During the eruption, magnetic reconnection continuously converts surrounding sheared arcades into newly reconnected twisted flux that merges with the pre-eruption MFR and continuously to build the erupting MFR. A component-resolved analysis of the Lorentz force work further shows that the newly reconnection flux contributes predominantly to the main acceleration phase. These results demonstrate that magnetic reconnection plays a central role throughout the entire lifecycle of the eruptive MFR: it builds the MFR, directly triggers its eruption, and provides the dominant driving of the eruption.
