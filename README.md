 - **Abstract**
 Scintillation arcs are sensitive probes of multipath propagation, offering a window into the distribution of scattering material in the interstellar medium. Contrary to the common observational finding that individual pulsars exhibit only a limited range of scintillation arc morphologies, we report that PSR~B0355+54, observed simultaneously at 2.25/8.60~GHz with the Shanghai Tianma Radio Telescope, displays nearly the full range of known arc patterns, many of which evolve rapidly. Tracking four arclets over 10~h at 8.60~GHz, including two displaced arclets, reveals a common evolutionary trend consistent with the line of sight sweeping across small-scale scattering structure, helping explain the rapid arc evolution.

 - **Abstract**
 The discovery of exoplanets around white dwarfs uniquely informs our understanding of planetary survival through stellar evolution. Common Envelope (CE) evolution is one of the proposed mechanisms that could shape the orbital architecture of these planets around stellar remnants. In this work, we predict the occurrence rate and orbital separation/planet mass distributions for planets that could form around white dwarfs through CE evolution.  - **Abstract**
 The rapid inward migration of low-mass planets remains a primary theoretical challenge in planet formation. While recent studies show that the solid component of protoplanetary disks can exert strong positive torques to counteract the negative gas torques, traditional static fixed-orbit models fail to capture the fully coupled dynamics of a migrating planet. In this work, we present 2D high-resolution, two-fluid hydrodynamical simulations using a comoving reference frame to track the self-consistent orbital evolution of embedded planets across a comprehensive six-dimensional parameter space. By allowing the planet to dynamically respond to disk-exerted torques, we identify a wide diversity of migration regimes, involving inward/outward migration and oscillatory radial confinement. We also find that massive cores can overcome dust-induced barriers, experiencing dust-mediated runaway outward or inward migration. Conversely, in dust-rich environments continuous gas-dust momentum exchange combined with planet torques dynamically regenerates co-orbital dust asymmetries, creating robust dynamical attractors where super-Earths undergo stable, oscillatory confinement, effectively halting inward drift without requiring pre-existing pressure bumps or disk substructures. This self-induced trapping mechanism could be relevant in the outer disk, beyond the water-ice line in environments with a enhanced local dust-to-gas ratio, where icy pebbles are moderately coupled to the gas. In low-metallicity or highly diffusive disks, planets generally adopt monotonic inward decay, apart from cases with outward migration. Ultimately, the diversity of these self-consistent trajectories demonstrates that static torque approximations are insufficient. Evaluating fully coupled kinematic feedback is essential for accurately predicting planetary survival and the final architecture of planetary systems.

For stars with masses sufficiently lower than the maximum mass, magnetic stresses redistribute angular momentum and drive the development of differential rotation, leading to a new quasi-stationary configuration that remains dynamically stable over several rotation periods. Magnetically-driven winds develop from the outer layers around the equatorial plane and remain baryon-matter dominated with magnetization ${\lesssim}0.2$. Over the simulated timescale, winds carry a total rest mass of order ${\sim}10^{-3}\mathrm{M}_\odot$ at outflow rates of up to a few $M_\odot/\mathrm{s}$. For stars close to (or beyond) the maximum-mass turning point along the stellar equilibrium sequence, strong magnetic fields trigger gravitational collapse by redistributing angular momentum away from the stellar core. The resulting black hole remnants are characterized by angular velocities up to ${\sim}49\%$ larger and angular momentum ${\lesssim}8\%$ smaller than those of the initial equilibrium stars. Our results show that extreme magnetic-field instabilities can significantly alter the structure of supramassive neutron stars, with possible implications for magnetars and long-lived binary neutron-star merger remnants.
