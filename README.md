 - **Abstract**
 We search for anisotropic cosmic birefringence using two years of data from the SPT-3G instrument on the South Pole Telescope. Anisotropic cosmic birefringence refers to a theorized dependence of the refractive index of the vacuum on the polarization of light that varies across the Universe in a statistically isotropic manner. Such an effect would manifest as a direction-dependent mixing of the $E$ and $B$ polarization modes of the cosmic microwave background (CMB), caused by rotation of the polarization angle as photons travel from the CMB last scattering surface to us. We present a measurement of the power spectrum of the rotation angle $\alpha$ in six bins across the angular multipole range $20 < L < 2048$, finding no evidence for cosmic birefringence with the spectrum consistent with zero. At 95% confidence, we set a new best upper limit on the amplitude of a scale-invariant birefringence power spectrum of $A_{\rm CB}<0.027$. Additionally, we place upper limits on a scale-dependent white noise model. We translate the scale-invariant upper limit to constraints on the axion coupling constant and primordial magnetic field strength, finding 95% confidence limits of $g_{\alpha\gamma} < 1.7\times 10^{-2}/H_I$ and $B_{\rm 1\,Mpc} < 9.6\,{\rm nG}$ respectively. We expect these limits to improve several-fold with the complete SPT-3G survey.

 - **Abstract**
 The star $\beta$ Pictoris is orbited by a dusty debris disk, within which planet formation is occurring. The disk extends far from the star such that molecules in the outer disk should freeze. We observed the $\beta$ Pictoris disk in the near-infrared using the James Webb Space Telescope. The spectrum shows features due to amorphous water ice (<7% crystallinity), and carbon dioxide ice in both the $^{12}$CO$_2$ and $^{13}$CO$_2$ isotopologues. #### Beam-pattern Prediction for Radio Antenna Arrays using Mutual Coupling and Machine Learning
 - **Authors:** Anna M. Heckel, Chris J. Pate, Jungmin Kim, Peter T. Timbie, Zongfu Yu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18968

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18968

 - **Abstract**
 Radio antennas are often used to form arrays, such as phased arrays and interferometer arrays. For most applications the beam patterns of these instruments must be accurately known. For phased arrays the array patterns are formed by coherently adding the embedded element patterns (EEPs) of the individual antennas in the array. For interferometers, the fundamental measurement is of visibilities, which depend on the products of pairs of EEPs. Unfortunately, in either case, for closely-spaced arrays, the EEPs can be distorted by mutual coupling between the antennas and can be very different from the isolated element patterns (IEPs), measured when the antennas are far apart. Mutual coupling is characterized by the scattering parameters for the array. Full-wave electromagnetic simulations can simulate the EEPs, including mutual coupling effects, but these simulations can be computationally expensive and require accurate knowledge of the design of the array elements, their placement, and the environment in which they are deployed. Directly measuring either the EEPs or the array patterns for instruments in the field is often impractical as well. In this paper we show that in cases where the scattering parameters between array elements can be measured, they can be used to infer the array patterns of a phased array. We demonstrate the training of a deep neural network to simulate array patterns when given: the design of the antennas, the locations of the antennas, the frequency of operation, and measurements of the scattering parameters of the array. As a demonstration for a simple model, we apply this approach to the simulation of the array patterns for a small phased array of three dipole antennas and evaluate the sensitivity of this procedure to uncertainties in measurements of the antenna positions and scattering parameters.

 - **Abstract**
 Large-scale core-collapse supernova simulations rely on tabulated nuclear equations of state (EOSs), whose memory requirements can become a practical constraint in massively parallel calculations. We explore a compact alternative by replacing the tabulated thermodynamic mapping with a neural-network surrogate, using the Lattimer--Swesty EOS as a representative reference model. The surrogate directly predicts pressure, sound speed, and effective adiabatic index from $(\log \rho, \log e, Y_e)$ and accurately reconstructs the nonlinear thermodynamic structure of the EOS, including its behavior across phase-transition regions.  - **Abstract**

Salt production is dominated by NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$, which is found to form predominantly during the H$_2$O-rich stage as its production is limited by the availability of NH$_3$. NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ is found to form later at the interface between the H$_2$O- and CO-rich ice when the formation of its precursor acid HNCO is enabled. Despite the variations explored, the total NH$_4^+$ abundance, particularly relative to H$_2$O, remains remarkably robust and consistent with the observed abundance. Depending on the variation, a redistribution between counterions HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ is observed.

 - **Abstract**
 The first generation of metal-free Population III (Pop~III) stars provides a possible formation channel for the black hole (BH) seeds in the early Universe. Their remnants may grow through gas accretion and mergers, producing gravitational wave (GW) signals detectable by future observatories. We develop a semi-analytical model of fragmentation in a primordial self-gravitating gas disk within minihaloes at $z > 15$, motivated by high-resolution cosmological simulations. The resulting Pop~III stars form around a central primary, some leaving massive BH remnants that migrate towards the central BH through dynamical friction from gas and dark matter, while the central BH grows through accretion and mergers. For a $10^6~{\rm M_\odot}$ halo at $z=20$, we explore how the initial mass function (IMF) and BH accretion efficiency affect detectability with the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) and the Einstein Telescope (ET). We find that both detectors can potentially reveal mergers of Pop III remnant BHs, but favor different astrophysical conditions. LISA pinpoints more massive central BHs ($\sim10^3$-$10^4 {\rm M_\odot}$), grown rapidly through mutual mergers alongside efficient gas accretion ($f_{\rm Edd}=1$), and a relatively top-heavy IMF ($\alpha=0.8$). In contrast, detectability with ET favors lower-mass BHs, with less efficient accretion ($f_{\rm Edd}=0.01$) and a steeper IMF ($\alpha=1.5$). These results highlight the potential of LISA and ET to probe Pop~III BH formation, growth, and mergers across different regions of parameter spaces, jointly constraining Pop~III star formation and early BH seed evolution.

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) predicts large-scale variations in exoplanet compositions with host star elemental abundances, reflecting the Milky Way's history of star formation and elemental enrichment from stellar feedback. We investigate the impact of GCE on the compositions of exoplanetary bodies using polluted white dwarfs (WDs), which probe the bulk elemental abundances of planetary debris. Using a literature sample of 70 polluted WDs, we calculate iron mass fractions for the accreted bodies and compare these to tracers of the WD progenitor's initial abundances, including age and kinematics. We demonstrate predicted trends using a sample of main sequence stars, and we show that these are generally reflected in the polluted WD sample. In particular, H-dominated WDs show preferentially larger iron mass fractions amongst younger and less dynamically excited systems. This trend is weaker amongst He-dominated WDs, and we show that this may be attributed to uncertainties in their observed phases of accretion.
